After only a couple of weeks of programming this game, I've had the hardest time with collision detection I've ever had. No RPG, physics simulation, or other program I've ever written has had collision detection that is this fundamentally broken. When you start the game in midair just above the ground, you sometimes, randomly, fall through the floor even though I've got an algorithm in place to stop you falling just before you even hit anything. Sometimes, you hit the ground and stay there, but the code doesn't register that as hitting the ground, and so you just vibrate in and out of the ground indefinitely. Sometimes, when you run off a platform at high speed, if you hit the side of another platform while still midair, you get stuck to it forever. Sometimes when you're running, you randomly glitch a single pixel into the floor and get stuck. Sometimes when you jump you glitch *the other way, through the ground.* At one point you had to left click after you landed from a jump or fall, otherwise you never registered as landing. Thankfully, I accidentally fixed that, and I still haven't the faintest clue what caused it.

I never expected this to be easy, but the level of glitchiness in the most innocent looking code is just ridiculous. I mean, collision detection in general is a very easy thing to break, just because you either have to predict whether two objects will collide and stop it happening, or find out after they've collided and try and reverse it; but it is really giving me a much harder time than I expected. (A note on collision detection: If your computer is only updating positions 60 times a second, the chances of in any one frame, two objects exactly touching but not glitching through each other, is so miniscule that you may as well forget it's possible. I've taken the first approach, stopping collisions before they happen, which is, as you may have guessed, easier said than done.)

I've also found that compared to the browser based games I used to make (with JavaScript and HTML5 Canvas,) C++ with hardware accelerated OpenGL rendering is just unbelievably faster. The same prototype that I have running in excess of 1000fps (when I disable v-sync, so high my counter can't measure any higher or it'd start dividing by zero and crashing,) if written in JavaScript, would probably run at around 100fps. I now struggle to understand how you can make a 2D game poorly optimised to the point of lagging on any computer from this century, unless it uses really fancy graphical effects or has extremely good enemy AIs. (Unless you're using a poorly made game engine, which usually will save boilerplate, but at the expense of massive performance hits, less customisation/configuration, and less available plaforms.)
