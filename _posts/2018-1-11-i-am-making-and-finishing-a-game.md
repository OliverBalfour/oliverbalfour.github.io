I've started making a game. I don't know what it is, or what it will be, but so far it's a side on tech demo with broken platformer physics and barely functional 'graphics.' Despite dealing with the same map maker, the same collision hitboxes and the same physics many times before, I've never combined them in this way, and I've found that collision detection and state management has been a real pain. Add to that that I'm writing this game in C++, a language I've only dabbled in before now, using SDL and OpenGL, libraries I'd only vaguely heard of until now, and I have a real challenge on my hands. I'm hoping, though, that through experimentation and trial-and-error that comes with working with a lower level programming language than the ones I'm used to, will help bring about a unique character for this game. (I say, as I am building the most generic game in history.)

For as long as I've been programming I've been creating games. I originally learned JavaScript to create an online multiplayer version of a role-playing game I made in primary school. But to this day, I've never finished one. Not one. Once, I came very close. I was making a real-time browser based strategy game similar to [generals.io](http://generals.io) and [kingz.io](http://kingz.io), except on a hexagonal map with different mechanics; and I finished the first version of the game, but never published it. I build up a to-do list of things that had to be done to get it online, but I was overwhelmed by the many little things like choosing a host, choosing a domain, designing an icon, making a tutorial, and building it 'hacker-proof.' But, every other time I've made a game, I've gotten bored after two weeks, or distracted, or I've had some other great game idea, or I take a break and forget about it, or I come up with some excuse to avoid having to do the annoying bits like making menus, by justifying to myself it's a 'tech demo' or a 'proof of concept.'

But a few weeks ago, around Christmas, I discovered a game called Gunpoint. My friend gave me a spare game key he had from a bundle of games he bought, and I thought it was a pretty neat little game. And, towards the end of the game, I thought &mdash; hey, I could make a game like that! I've made plenty of stuff like that, I'd just need a good artist &mdash; oh wait... But I looked at the game's website and found the developer's devblog, and found out the dev made the game in GameMaker on weekends as a hobby, with no prior programming experience. I also encountered [this](http://makegames.tumblr.com/post/1136623767/finishing-a-game) little gem of a blog post, by the creator of Spelunky, which perfectly matched my feelings at the time. So, I decided to make a game. At the time I'd been messing around with C++ and OpenGL with little luck. I spend three whole days trying simply to render text to the screen, and just got segmentation fault after black screen after compile error. I decided to use C++, and SDL's 2D hardware accelerated drawing API, because it supported drawing text to the screen without any more wasted hours.

After a week or so of building up a basic class heirarchy, I decided that I would continue to build this game for the entire of 2018. No other programming allowed, except for writing automated scripts or other trivial things. So, I've started a development blog, both to foster commitment to this probably-doomed-to-fail project, and to see if others can learn from my success or more likely failure once I am done.
