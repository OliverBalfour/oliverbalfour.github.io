I haven't had much time to work on my game in the past few weeks, now that I'm back at school. I'm still stuck on collision detection and basic physics at the moment, although instead of glitching through the floor the player now gets stuck in walls when they hit them in midair. I know how to fix all of this, except now that I have studying to focus on I'm finding it very difficult to concentrate on programming. All I really need to fix this all is a two hour chunk of time on a weekend with no distractions and no other work left to do, and then I can get right into making this game unique (or at least less generic,) but until then I'll just have to brainstorm ideas in my free time.

I don't really want to write down a massive specification for exactly what my game should be, how many boss monsters it should have and where, and what gameplay mechanics it must have, as I've learnt from past experience that that's a terrible way to work, overloading myself with an endless list of ideas until I burn out. Instead, I've realised that if I just come up with ideas for the various types of enemies the player will encounter, just simple ones without any special abilities or game mechanics, I'll have a list of easily implementable ideas that I can toy with once I get the time to finish the physics.

I'll have to continunally remind myself not to go overboard and create an elaborate plan to burn myself out as quickly as possible, and that there'll still be things like menus and loading screens and UI to do, so I should probably consider implementing that cool flying [REDACTED] or that clever little [REDACTED] as something of a treat, as occasional respite from the endless boatloads of boring work like menus and working out how the hell to publish this thing anyway.

Whenever I've made games in the past, I've had some cool idea, and I've just built it, no menus, no graphics, just a basic prototype which only has things that are fun to code included, making for a very patchy 'gaming' experience, if it could even be called that. Usually I get bored of making a game when I cannot possibly go any further without adding a menu screen or something to give the game a backbone to rest upon. I guess what I'll try and do different this time is do the boring things first, and save the best until last.
